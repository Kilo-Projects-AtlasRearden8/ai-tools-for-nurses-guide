5. Free AI models you can use today

You do not need to pay, and you do not need to be technical. These are free ways to try AI. Models change often, so treat this as a starting point, not a permanent list.

A. Browser-based free options (easiest to start)

  • Major AI companies usually offer a free tier you can use in a web browser or phone app. Look for “free” or “sign up with Google/Apple” options.
  • Some let you use the model without creating an account at all.
  • Trade-off: your conversations may be stored by the provider — read the privacy policy, and never paste patient data.

B. Local-first options (runs on your device)

  • Ollama (ollama.com) — free, open-source tool that runs models on your own computer. Nothing has to leave your device.
  • llama.cpp and other open-source runners — free, for more technical users.
  • Trade-off: you need a fairly modern computer, and setup takes a little time. Good if privacy matters most to you.

Step-by-step: try a browser-based free model

  1. Pick a well-known AI provider with a free web tier.
  2. Create an account if required (use an email you don’t mind sharing).
  3. Start a new chat.
  4. Copy one of the example prompts from this guide and paste it in.
  5. Read the answer carefully — AI can be wrong.
  6. When you’re done, you can delete the chat in the provider’s settings.

Step-by-step: try a local-first model (Ollama)

  1. Go to ollama.com and download the free app for your computer.
  2. Install and open it.
  3. Choose a small model to start (the site will suggest one).
  4. Type a prompt in the chat window.
  5. Because it runs locally, your conversation stays on your machine.

Privacy checklist before you start

  • ✓ No patient names, photos, MRNs, or identifying details — ever.
  • ✓ I read the tool’s privacy policy (or at least know it may store chats).
  • ✓ My facility allows AI use for what I’m doing.
  • ✓ I will verify anything clinical before relying on it.
  • ✓ I won’t enter passwords, credit cards, or other secrets.

Red lines: Free does not mean private. Assume anything you type could be seen by the provider. Never paste patient data. AI models can be confidently wrong about doses, symptoms, and clinical facts — verify everything against trusted references and your clinical judgment.
