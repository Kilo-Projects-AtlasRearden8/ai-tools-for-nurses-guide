1. Shift prep with AI

Use a free AI tool to get ready for your shift — reviewing conditions, medications, handoff questions, and your own prep checklist. Think of it as a study buddy that never gets tired, not as a clinical decision-maker.

Before you start

  • Open a free AI tool (see Free AI models).
  • Have nothing patient-specific nearby that you would be tempted to paste.
  • Keep your facility’s policy on AI use in mind — some facilities restrict it entirely.

Step 1: Pick your focus for the shift

Tell the AI what kind of shift you’re working so the prep matches.

Example prompt: “I’m a registered nurse working a 12-hour night shift on a medical-surgical floor. I’d like a short shift-prep checklist covering common conditions, meds to review, and handoff questions. Keep it in plain English and education-only — no patient data.”

Step 2: Review conditions you might see

Ask for plain-language refreshers on conditions common on your unit. The AI can outline typical signs, symptoms, and what nurses commonly watch for — you verify everything against your facility’s references.

Example prompt: “Give me a short, plain-English refresher on congestive heart failure: what it is, common signs and symptoms, and what nurses typically monitor. Use bullet points, about a 6th-grade reading level, education only.”

Step 3: Review medications

Ask about common medications on your unit: what they’re for, typical side effects, and what nurses commonly monitor. Always double-check against your drug reference and pharmacy — AI can be wrong about doses.

Example prompt: “I work med-surg. List 8 common medications on a med-surg floor, what each is used for, 2–3 common side effects to watch for, and one thing nurses commonly monitor. No doses, education only.”

Step 4: Practice handoff questions

Use AI to draft questions you might ask at shift handoff or that the oncoming nurse might ask you. Practice answering out loud.

Example prompt: “List 8 good questions to ask during a nursing shift handoff on a med-surg unit, plus 4 questions the incoming nurse might ask me. Plain English.”

Step 5: Write a practice SBAR

SBAR (Situation, Background, Assessment, Recommendation) is a common handoff format. Ask the AI to show you an example structure — using a made-up scenario with no real patient details.

Example prompt: “Show me a short example SBAR for a nurse calling a provider about a patient, using a completely made-up patient with no real names or dates. Label each section: Situation, Background, Assessment, Recommendation. Education only.”

Step 6: Build your own checklist

Ask the AI to format everything into one short checklist you can screenshot, print, or copy into your notes app. Keep it generic — no unit-specific patient info.

Example prompt: “Combine the above into a one-page shift-prep checklist for a med-surg night shift: conditions to review, medications to review, handoff questions, and a personal “before I leave home” list. Plain English, education only, no patient data.”

Red lines: Never paste a real patient’s name, room number, diagnosis, or any identifying details. AI outputs can be wrong about doses, indications, and clinical facts — always verify with your drug reference, facility policy, and clinical judgment. AI does not replace your assessment of a real patient.
