2. Patient education with AI

Patients understand and remember more when instructions are short, plain, and focused on what to do. AI is good at rewriting clinical language into everyday language — about a 6th-grade reading level — so you can review it and use it as a starting point.

Ground rules

  • Never paste real patient details into an AI tool.
  • Always review and sign off on anything before it reaches a patient.
  • Prefer your facility’s approved education materials first; use AI only to adapt or supplement.
  • Use teach-back: ask the patient to explain it back in their own words.

Step 1: Explain a condition in plain language

Example prompt: “Explain type 2 diabetes in plain English for a patient who just received a new diagnosis. About a 6th-grade reading level. Use short sentences, bullet points, and include: what it is, common symptoms, and why treatment matters. Add a line saying this is general information and the patient should ask their care team about their own situation. Education only — not medical advice.”

Step 2: Draft discharge instructions

Ask the AI to organize discharge information into plain sections: medicines, warning signs, follow-up, and questions to ask. You fill in the patient-specific details yourself, on paper or in your facility’s system — never in the AI tool.

Example prompt: “Draft a plain-language discharge instruction outline for a patient going home after pneumonia. Sections: medicines, activity, warning signs to call about, follow-up appointments, and questions to ask the care team. About a 6th-grade reading level. Include a reminder to call 911 for emergency symptoms. Education only.”

Step 3: Draft medication teaching points

Draft short teaching points for common medications: what it’s for, how to take it, common side effects, and when to call. Verify every detail against your drug reference before sharing.

Example prompt: “Write 4 short teaching points for a patient starting lisinopril: what it does, how to take it, common side effects, and when to call the clinic. Plain English, no doses, education only.”

Step 4: Simplify existing instructions

Paste facility-approved text (no patient details) and ask the AI to make it shorter and simpler.

Example prompt: “Rewrite the following wound-care instructions in plain English, about a 6th-grade reading level, using short sentences and bullet points. Keep all safety warnings. [paste approved facility text only, no patient details]”

Step 5: Prepare teach-back questions

Ask the AI for questions you can use to check understanding without sounding like a test.

Example prompt: “List 5 friendly teach-back questions a nurse can ask to check that a patient understands new medication instructions, without sounding like a test.”

Red lines: Never paste patient names, photos, MRNs, or details that could identify someone. AI can invent facts — verify doses, side effects, and warnings against your drug reference and clinical judgment. Give patients your facility’s approved materials whenever one exists.
