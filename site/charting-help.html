3. Charting help with AI

AI can turn rough notes into tidy drafts, shorten wordy paragraphs, and show you how common note formats are structured. The key rule: you write from your own memory and observations, de-identified, and you review every line before anything goes in the chart.

Golden rule: never paste patient data

Do not paste names, dates of birth, MRNs, room numbers, dates of service, photos, or any combination of details that could identify a patient. Work from de-identified bullets you wrote yourself:

  • Instead of “Mr. Smith, room 412,” write “patient A.”
  • Instead of “05/14/2026 at 1400,” write “this afternoon.”
  • Keep it generic: “post-op day 2, abdominal incision.”

Step 1: Write your own bullets first

Jot down what you did and observed, on paper or in a private note — never in the AI tool with identifiers.

Your notes (paper or private app): Patient A post-op day 2. Ambulated 3 times today with assist. Incision clean, dry, intact. Pain 3/10 after acetaminophen. IV site fine. Diet advanced to regular. Family present and taught about incision care.

Step 2: Ask the AI to organize into a note format

Example prompt: “Turn these de-identified nursing notes into a SOAP note (Subjective, Objective, Assessment, Plan). Use plain professional nursing language. No patient names or dates. Education only. [paste de-identified bullets only]”

Step 3: Choose a format your facility uses

Common formats: SOAP, DAP (Data, Assessment, Plan), SOAPIE (adds Intervention and Evaluation), and narrative. Ask the AI to show the structure so you can match your facility’s template.

Example prompt: “What is the difference between SOAP, DAP, and SOAPIE nursing notes? Give one short made-up example for each, no real patient details, education only.”

Step 4: Tighten wordy documentation

Ask the AI to shorten a paragraph you wrote while keeping all clinically relevant facts.

Example prompt: “Shorten this nursing note paragraph but keep all clinically relevant facts. Professional tone, no fluff. [paste your own de-identified draft only]”

Step 5: Review before anything goes in the chart

Read the AI draft line by line. Ask: Is it accurate? Is anything missing? Is anything added that didn’t happen? Remove anything you didn’t observe or do. Then enter it yourself in your facility’s system.

Red lines: AI can hallucinate — it may invent vitals, medications, or events that never happened. Only you can verify what is true. Never let AI write directly into your EHR. Check your facility policy: some facilities prohibit AI in charting entirely. When in doubt, chart it yourself.
