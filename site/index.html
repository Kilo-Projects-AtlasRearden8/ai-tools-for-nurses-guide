About this guide

AI tools for nurses and healthcare workers — education only

This manual is written for nurses and other healthcare workers who are curious about AI but have never used it, or who have tried it once and felt overwhelmed. No coding, no math, and nothing you have to pay for. Every tool mentioned has a free option.

Important: This guide is for general education only. It is not medical advice, not a substitute for clinical judgment, and not a substitute for your facility’s policies or required training. Always follow your employer’s rules and scope of practice. Never paste real patient names, dates of birth, medical record numbers, photos, or any other patient information into an AI tool. When in doubt, leave patient details out.

What’s inside

1. Shift prep

Get ready for your shift with free AI: build a prep checklist, review common conditions and medications, practice handoff questions, and write SBAR notes — without pasting patient data.

2. Patient education

Draft plain-language explanations (about a 6th-grade reading level), discharge-instruction drafts, and medication teaching points for your own review. A clinician always checks the final words.

3. Charting help

Turn rough bullets into organized notes (SOAP / DAP / SOAPIE), tighten wordy documentation, and learn what belongs in a note — and what never does.

4. Scheduling

Plan your personal shifts, block sleep around night shifts, manage rotations, and organize your week — for your own time management, not for staffing decisions.

5. Free AI models

Where to find free models you can use today, including options that run on your own device so nothing leaves your computer.

How to use this guide

  1. Read one section at a time. Each section has step-by-step instructions.
  2. Open a free AI tool in your browser (see Free AI models) and try the example prompts exactly as written.
  3. Change the examples to fit your own role and facility.
  4. Print a cheat sheet from Cheat sheets and keep it at your workstation or in your bag.
