Safety & privacy rules

This is education only — not medical advice

Nothing in this guide is medical advice, a diagnosis, a treatment recommendation, or a substitute for clinical judgment. It does not replace your facility’s policies, required training, scope of practice, or your clinical assessment of a real patient. When AI and your judgment disagree, trust your judgment and your facility’s references.

Never paste patient data

Never enter into an AI tool: patient names, photos, dates of birth, medical record numbers, room numbers, dates of service, diagnoses, or any combination of details that could identify someone. Work from de-identified, generic notes you wrote yourself.

Why this matters

  • AI conversations may be stored by the tool’s provider.
  • Sharing patient details may violate HIPAA and your facility’s policies.
  • Once something is pasted into an AI tool, you cannot take it back.

AI can be wrong

  • Models can invent facts, doses, medications, and events.
  • Always verify clinical information against your drug reference, facility policy, and clinical judgment.
  • Never let AI write directly into your EHR or chart.

Before you use AI at work

  1. Read your facility’s policy on AI and on personal device use.
  2. Check whether AI in charting is allowed — many facilities prohibit it.
  3. Use approved patient-education materials first.
  4. Keep patient data out — always.
  5. When in doubt, do it yourself without AI.
